Five projects, each written up with the constraint I was given, the decisions I made, and what shipped.

Incident Console redesign

Northbridge's on-call engineers were triaging alerts in a feed sorted by timestamp, which meant a low-severity alert from thirty seconds ago could bury a critical one from three minutes ago. I redesigned the console around severity and ownership: alerts group by the service they belong to, escalate visually as they age unacknowledged, and default to showing only what's assigned to the viewer's team.

The hardest part wasn't the layout — it was convincing the team to cut fields. The original view surfaced eleven columns of metadata per alert; the shipped version surfaces three, with the rest available on expand. Mean time-to-acknowledge dropped 38% in the following quarter, measured against the same alert volume.

Ledger reconciliation tool

A six-person finance-ops team was reconciling two payment ledgers by hand across roughly forty spreadsheet tabs each week. I designed a guided reconciliation queue: the system pre-matches transactions it's confident about and surfaces only the discrepancies that need a human decision, one at a time, with the relevant history alongside each one.

I spent the first two weeks just shadowing the reconciliation process before sketching anything, which is where the "one discrepancy at a time" structure came from — the team's biggest complaint wasn't the math, it was losing their place across forty open tabs.

Field logistics planner

Kestrel's maintenance crews worked in areas with patchy or no signal, which ruled out most off-the-shelf routing software. I designed a route planner and crew-tracking app that works fully offline, queues updates locally, and syncs opportunistically — dispatchers plan routes online, crews follow them without needing a connection.

The interesting constraint was trust: crews needed to know at a glance whether their view was live or stale. I used a persistent, always-visible sync indicator rather than burying connection status in a settings menu, which came directly out of field interviews where crews described being burned by silently outdated data before.

Internal design system

Before the incident console redesign, Northbridge's internal tools had no shared component library — every team's dashboard used slightly different spacing, color, and interaction patterns, which made cross-team tools feel like separate products. I audited eleven internal tools, extracted the patterns worth keeping, and built a documented component library on top of them.

I treated accessibility as a launch requirement rather than a follow-up pass: every component shipped with keyboard interaction specs and contrast-checked color tokens from day one, which made the later incident console work considerably faster.

Self-serve onboarding flow

A B2B analytics product had a 40-minute, sales-assisted setup process that was becoming a bottleneck as the company moved toward self-serve signups. I redesigned setup as a three-step flow that front-loads the one integration step that actually blocks value, deferring nice-to-have configuration until after the user sees real data.

Self-serve activation (accounts reaching first meaningful dashboard view) rose from 22% to 51% over two release cycles.
